News

Each released version of the book has an entry here that says what changed since the version before it, so you can check whether something you read has since been corrected. Earlier versions stay online and can be opened from the version menu in the sidebar.

Version 0.1.2 (27 November 2026)

Released 27 November 2026. Every changed line since version 0.1.1: compare on GitHub.

Changes since version 0.1.1:

  • 27 November 2026. Chapter 5, The Metropolis Algorithm, revised after student feedback. There is a new figure of the transition density of a continuous chain, the section on several parameters now starts from the sleep survey, and the bivariate Normal figure marks which of the first 80 proposals were accepted. A quiz answer that no longer had a quiz was removed.
  • 27 November 2026. The old Chapter 6 on Metropolis–Hastings and adaptive proposals is gone. In its place are two chapters, both online now. Chapter 6, Adaptive Metropolis, lets the sampler tune its own step size and explains the Robbins–Monro procedure behind that. Chapter 7, The Metropolis–Hastings Algorithm, drops the assumption of a symmetric proposal, with a log-Normal proposal for a standard deviation and the independence sampler as the two examples.
  • 27 November 2026. The split pushes the chapters still in preparation up by one: Gibbs sampling is now Chapter 8, variable selection 9, model averaging 10, and Hamiltonian Monte Carlo 11. Mentions of them in Chapters 2 to 5 and in the appendices were updated.

Version 0.1.1 (20 November 2026)

Released 20 November 2026. Every changed line since version 0.1: compare on GitHub.

Changes since version 0.1:

  • 30 October 2026. Welcome page: who the book is for and how it teaches, rewritten.
  • 30 October 2026. Appendices: updated setup instructions and package lists, revised notation, and a step-by-step derivation of the variance of a dependent average.
  • 30 October 2026. Chapters 4 and 5: clearer exercise instructions and stronger simulation comparisons.
  • 20 November 2026. Chapter 5 has been substantially revised to develop the Metropolis algorithm step by step: constructing and implementing the sampler, explaining why it works, and showing why a valid sampler can still explore poorly. The revised step-size section connects acceptance rates to effective sample size and explains how to use them when tuning the sampler.
